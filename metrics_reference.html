Three families, three orderings

Surface overlap, learned semantic adequacy and clinical fidelity are computed over the same outputs. No two of them agree on which system is best.

hymt2-30b-a3b is first on COMET, sixth on BLEU among the eleven systems COMET scored, and second-worst of twelve on clinical errors. A learned metric trained on human adequacy judgements produces a third ordering and still does not track clinical risk, so the divergence is not an artefact of n-gram matching.

What the metrics do to one sentence

The same reference, five candidate translations. Three of them would change patient management; one is a harmless rewording.

The harmless paraphrase scores worst on all three surface metrics. Dropping “No” from “No pleural effusion” costs 15.5 BLEU; saying the same true thing in different words costs 47. COMET reproduces the blind spot rather than fixing it: it scores the dropped negation 0.911 against 0.981 for the correct translation.

Single-pass scores

PARROT German radiology reports, 296 documents, scored per document. COMET is Unbabel/wmt22-comet-da over the same outputs, for the twelve systems it was run on.

crit% counts documents with any critical finding; words% counts only negation and laterality findings and leaves out number and measurement findings, which are two thirds of the critical findings on this corpus. Both come from one detector version, rescored on 2026-09-29, because the terminology detector changed after the July runs and mixing versions had made one system look eight times better on terminology than it was.

DeepL ran on the free API plan (EN-US target). It and translategemma-27b have no COMET score. DeepL writes 5-mm for a compound modifier in 5 of 296 reports, and the number detector reads that as a mismatch against 5 mm. Normalising the hyphen would clear 2 of its 76 critical documents (25.68% to 25.00%); the only other system affected is hymt2-30b-a3b (−0.34 points). words% is not affected.

Round-trip scores

Ten DE→EN→DE cycles, twenty translation passes, 20 reports. English steps are scored against the human English reference and German steps against the original German; both anchors are fixed, so cycle 1 equals the ordinary single-pass evaluation.

Front-loaded is the share of the total BLEU loss that occurs in the first round trip. It averages 77%: translation reaches a fixed point rather than decaying without bound.

Interventions

Seven attempts to improve clinical fidelity. The distractor arm injects the same number of terms that do not occur in the document, which is what separates “the terms helped” from “the block helped”. adopted is the share of injected terms that appear in the output; orphan is the share of adopted terms the human reference does not use.

Not one intervention produced a significant improvement in clinical error rate. Every McNemar test returned p ≥ 0.25. The single directional positive was the smallest, validated glossary — 11.9% against a 12.8% control, three documents out of 109. The two mined banks show why the terminology route is closed: the terms the models already get right are mined accurately, the terms with headroom are mined badly, and against the distractor control the headroom bank is worth −0.12 BLEU (p = 0.45) — no signal at all.

The ceiling on the clinical metric

Before reading any intervention result, it is worth knowing how much of the critical-error rate a terminology or committee method could reach even in principle. On the 100 held-out documents, most of it is unreachable by construction.

Why one reference is not enough

PARROT gives one English translation per report, and on the vocabulary a glossary targets the corpus does not agree with itself. Beurteilung is a section header, so its rendering can be counted exactly by aligning the impression-role header on both sides. Every form below is what radiologists write — this project’s own section classifier treats them as one role.

A single-reference lexical metric cannot separate a wrong term from a correct synonym the reference happens not to use — and on high-frequency report vocabulary that is the common case, not the edge case. This is a property of the corpus, not of any system measured against it.

Reading notes

Metric signatures

A BLEU number without a signature is not reproducible; different tokenisers move it by several points. Corpus scores use sacreBLEU’s corpus scorers, never the mean of sentence scores.

Resolution

The critical-error rate is a share of documents, so its resolution is set by the sample: 5 points at n=20, 2.5 at n=40, roughly 0.9 at n=109. Differences of one step are not interpretable. Treat BLEU differences under ~1 point as noise, and never compare BLEU across test sets.

Two known biases

Under-translation flatters the clinical metric. A detector cannot flag a measurement that was never emitted. opus posts the lowest single-pass error rate while its output shrinks from 599 to 506 characters.

Detector recall is unmeasured. Three systems rendered BWK 12 (twelfth thoracic vertebra) as L12 (twelfth lumbar) and no detector fired. The true error rate is higher than the reported one by an unknown margin.
